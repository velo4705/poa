# Code of Conduct

POA is a tool for porting software. It stays useful when the people around
it are trustworthy. This page explains what that means here.

## Our pledge

POA operates in a space where IP, licensing, and quality claims are easy to
muddle. We commit to:

- **Honesty over hype.** We do not market "no quality loss" we cannot deliver.
- **Evidence over enthusiasm.** Green CI beats a good story.
- **Attribution where it's owed.** Open-source inputs are credited in port
  reports; derivative work respects licenses.
- **No circumvention.** No DRM stripping, anti-cheat bypass, or piracy
  enablement — and no contributions that help others do so.

## Expected behavior

- Courteous and specific technical questions, with evidence attached.
- Port reports include the failing input and the platform attempted, not
  "it doesn't work".
- Disagreements are about code and design, not people.
- Newcomers get the benefit of the doubt for one question per comment.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Posting source-generated artifacts as if they were original work.
- Claiming a port is "verified" when it is only "built".
- Asking for or sharing circumvention techniques (DRM/anti-cheat/console SDK
  workarounds) inside this repo or associated channels.
- Harassment, or discrimination of any kind.
- Leaking security details of software not yet publicly disclosed.

## Reporting

Report violations via the GitHub issue tracker, labeled `conduct`. For sensitive
reports, email the maintainer listed in the repo's profile rather than opening
a public issue. The maintainer will acknowledge, investigate, and respond
privately within a reasonable window.

## Scope

Applies in the repo, its issue tracker, PR review, and any other official
channels for this project. It does not regulate what you do elsewhere on the
internet.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may moderate, redact, lock, or remove content and contributor
access for violations. Decisions are documented privately to the reporter;
repeated abuse leads to bans.
